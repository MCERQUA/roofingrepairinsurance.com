## The Job-Site Theft Reality for Repair Roofers

Roofing repair crews carry their tools to job sites and leave them overnight. Storm restoration contractors stage gear at active repair sites for days at a time. Tarps, nail guns, compressors, generators — it's a toolkit that's mobile, valuable, and regularly left in trucks or at job sites.

Job-site and vehicle theft is one of the top insurance claims for roofing contractors. And it's also one of the most frequently uninsured losses — because most contractors assume their business insurance covers it when it typically doesn't.

## What Standard Commercial Property Misses

Commercial property insurance covers property at your **listed location** — your office, shop, or warehouse. Once your tools leave that address, standard commercial property generally stops covering them.

Common exclusions and limitations:

- **Off-premises sublimit:** Some policies have a small off-premises sublimit (e.g., $2,500) that covers tools temporarily away from the listed location. For a roofing repair crew with $30,000 in tools, a $2,500 sublimit is essentially no coverage.
- **No job-site coverage:** Tools at an active or inactive job site are almost always excluded from standard commercial property.
- **Vehicle limitation:** Tools stolen from vehicles are excluded unless the policy has a specific inland transit or vehicle theft provision.

The only way to properly cover tools at job sites and in transit is with inland marine — specifically, a contractor's equipment or tools and equipment policy.

## How Inland Marine Covers Repair Contractor Tools

A properly structured inland marine policy for roofing repair contractors covers:

**Tools at job sites.** Whether you're on an active repair site, left tools overnight on a weekend call, or staged gear at a storm-damage site between crew rotations — inland marine covers theft and accidental damage at the job site.

**Tools in transit.** Tools damaged or stolen while being transported in trucks and trailers are covered. This includes theft from locked vehicles (subject to the forced-entry requirement most policies include).

**Tools at your shop or storage.** Inland marine also covers tools at your listed location, often overlapping with commercial property — but with higher limits and fewer exclusions.

**Tarps and weatherproofing equipment.** Storm restoration contractors who carry significant tarp inventory should make sure tarps and tarp-securing equipment are explicitly included. They often are, but check the policy language.

**Rented equipment.** If you rent generators, compressors, or lift equipment for specific repair jobs, that rented gear can often be added to your inland marine. You may be on the hook for damage to rented equipment regardless of what your credit card's coverage offers.

## Scheduled vs. Blanket Coverage

Inland marine tools policies are structured either as **scheduled** or **blanket** (or a combination):

- **Scheduled:** Each item is listed with its own value. Better for high-value individual pieces — a commercial nail gun at $3,500, a generator at $5,000, a compressor at $8,000.
- **Blanket:** A total limit covers all tools without itemizing each piece. More convenient for general hand tools and small equipment.

For repair contractors with a mix of high-value and standard tools, a combination approach — blanket for general tools, scheduled for key equipment — usually works best.

## The Forced-Entry Requirement

Most inland marine policies require evidence of forced entry for theft from a locked vehicle or secured storage. This means:

- If your truck was locked and the window was broken to steal tools: covered.
- If your truck was unlocked and tools were taken: claim may be denied.

Always lock your vehicles, especially overnight at job sites. And when filing a claim for vehicle theft, document the forced entry — photos of the broken window, police report noting forced entry.

## Filing a Tools Theft Claim

If tools are stolen from a job site or vehicle:

1. **File a police report.** This is required by virtually all inland marine carriers. Do it immediately — don't wait until you're back at the office.
2. **Document what was taken.** Write down everything stolen with as much detail as you have — model, serial number, estimated replacement cost. The more detail, the faster the claim is processed.
3. **Notify your insurance agent same day.** Most policies require prompt reporting of theft claims. Delays can complicate the claim.
4. **Replacement cost basis.** Make sure your inland marine covers tools at replacement cost, not actual cash value. A 5-year-old nail gun at ACV might pay you $200. At replacement cost, it pays you what a new equivalent tool costs.
