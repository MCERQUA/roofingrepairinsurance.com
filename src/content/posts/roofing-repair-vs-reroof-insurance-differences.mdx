## The Same Business, Different Risk Profiles

Most roofing repair contractors do both: patch and repair work on a daily basis, and full re-roofs when a roof is beyond repair or a customer wants a clean start. From a business perspective, it's all roofing. From an insurance perspective, the two types of work have meaningfully different risk characteristics.

Understanding the differences matters because your GL and workers' comp need to reflect your actual scope of work — not just one side of it.

## How Repair Work Differs Insurably from Re-Roofing

**Volume of completed-operations exposures.** Repair work generates high completed-ops exposure volume — many jobs per day or week, each with its own long-tail liability. Re-roofing generates fewer, larger completed-ops exposures. High-volume repair contractors accumulate completed-ops risk faster.

**Documentation and scope clarity.** A full re-roof has a clear before/after: old roofing removed, new system installed. A repair has a narrower scope, which means disputes about what was repaired and who's responsible for adjacent damage are more common. This makes accurate scope-of-work documentation even more important for repair contractors.

**Prior defect exposure.** When you re-roof, you remove the old system and the prior contractor's work. When you repair, you're working on and around the prior contractor's installation — and you may inadvertently be blamed for pre-existing conditions you didn't create.

**Material handling and storage.** Re-roofing involves staging and storing larger quantities of material on site. Inland marine (tools and equipment) policies that cover materials in your care, custody, and control matter more for full re-roof operations with significant on-site material staging.

## What Changes in the Insurance Program

**GL limits and aggregate sizing.** High-volume repair contractors may need a larger completed-ops aggregate than a re-roofing contractor doing fewer, higher-value projects. The total exposure accumulation is different even if the per-project risk is similar.

**Workers' comp classification.** Both repair and re-roofing work typically fall under roofing class codes, but some states distinguish between repair work and re-roofing for classification purposes. Get specific guidance on classification for your actual mix of work.

**Builders risk.** Full re-roofing projects sometimes require builders risk coverage, especially on commercial properties or when the project involves significant structural elements. Pure repair work rarely triggers builders risk requirements.

**Inland marine scope.** Re-roofing operations often have larger material staging on site — pallets of shingles, membrane rolls, insulation. Making sure your inland marine covers materials in your care, custody, and control at job sites (not just your tools) matters more for re-roof contractors.

## The Certificate Question

When a property owner or GC asks for your certificate of insurance, they don't always distinguish between repair and re-roofing work. But the certificate requirements may differ:

- A homeowner hiring you for a repair may just want to see GL and workers' comp at basic limits.
- A commercial property manager hiring you for a re-roof may require higher limits, umbrella coverage, an additional insured endorsement, and sometimes builders risk.

Make sure your policy is flexible enough to accommodate both types of certificates — and that your agent can endorse your policy quickly to meet project-specific certificate requirements.

## If You Do Both

The most practical solution for contractors who do both repair work and full re-roofs is to build a program that covers both clearly:

- Describe your work mix accurately to your carrier: "We do approximately 60% repair work and 40% full re-roofing."
- Make sure your GL is occurrence-based with completed-ops coverage for both work types.
- Keep your tools and equipment coverage current for both the small repair tools and the larger staging equipment used in re-roof projects.
- If you take on commercial re-roofing work, add umbrella and make sure your certificate is compliant with commercial project requirements.
